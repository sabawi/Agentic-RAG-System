Coffee originated in the East African territories of Ethiopia and Yemen around 1400, where it was used by Arab Sufi monks for staying awake during midnight prayers. Over time, it became a global commodity and played a crucial role in building empires and fueling an industrial revolution. The Industrial Revolution itself was a monumental shift in how people lived, with workdays becoming longer due to electrification and the rise of factories. Coffee consumption increased across Europe during this period, helping to boost human productivity and drive modernization.

Before the Industrial Revolution, workdays were regulated by natural light, and people drank alcohol throughout the day to cope with physical hardship. However, as industrialization progressed, coffee became a popular alternative for staying awake and powering through long work hours. The rise of coffee as an everyday staple can be traced back centuries, through revolutions, prohibitions, and the dawn of the Enlightenment.

The influence of coffee extends beyond just the food industry and catering. It has been a key factor in the historical development of societies, playing a role in building and dismantling empires, fueling industrial revolutions, and even contributing to human exploitation, slavery, and violent civil wars.
